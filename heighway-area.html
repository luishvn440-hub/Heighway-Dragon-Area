Rectangle construction

Traverse the dragon from tail to head. Each consecutive pair of segments forms two sides of a square (since every turn is ±90°). That square is drawn and counted.

At even iteration k: squares align with the axis grid. At odd k: squares are rotated 45°.

Area formula

At iteration k there are 2k segments and therefore 2k−1 squares. Each segment has length (1/√2)k, so each square has area (1/√2)2k = 1/2k.

Ak = 2k−1 × 1/2k = 1/2

This holds for every k — the area is always exactly ½.

Tiling the plane

The squares from any iteration can tile the plane with 4 rotated copies (0°, 90°, 180°, 270°), just as the dragon curve itself tiles the plane.

Four copies × area ½ = total area 2 per fundamental domain, confirming the tiling fills space exactly.

Why area = ½

As k → ∞ both the dragon and the square approximations tile the plane. The dragon's area equals the limit of Ak = ½. Equivalently, the 2 IFS maps each scale by 1/√2, so 2 × (1/√2)² = 1, giving area = 1/(2×1) = ½.
